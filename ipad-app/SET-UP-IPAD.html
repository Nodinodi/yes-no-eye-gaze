YES / NO app – iPad set-up

Do this once. It takes about 15 minutes.

1. Check the iPad

  • Settings → General → Software Update: iPadOS 18 or newer.
  • Settings → Accessibility: there must be an Eye Tracking item. If it is missing, this iPad cannot do eye tracking.

2. Put the iPad in place

  • Fix the iPad firmly (wheelchair or table mount), about 45 cm from his face.
  • The front camera should be level with his eyes. Avoid bright light behind him.

3. Turn on Eye Tracking

  • Settings → Accessibility → Eye Tracking → on.
  • He follows the dot with his eyes to calibrate. (This repeats every time Eye Tracking is turned on.)
  • Smoothing: move a little to the right if the pointer shakes.
  • Snap to Item: off. The buttons are big, and snapping could pull his gaze from the empty area onto YES or NO.

4. Dwell Control (look and hold = tap)

  • Dwell Control: on. Set the time to about 2 seconds (anything from 1.5 to 2.5 seconds is fine). The app is built for this: after each choice it waits longer than one dwell. If he needs a longer time than 2.5 seconds, ask for the app to be adjusted.
  • Leave Fallback Action on Tap.
  • Hot Corners: set every corner to None. The big YES/NO buttons reach near the corners.

5. Keep the screen on and the sound up

  • Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → Never. He cannot wake a dark screen with his eyes.
  • Turn the volume up, and make sure Silent Mode is off (Control Centre), so the spoken answer can be heard.

6. Install the app

  • Follow INSTALL-ON-IPAD.md (in the same folder as this guide): install the free Swift Playgrounds app, copy the YES NO app to the iPad, open it and tap Run.
  • Make the app fill the whole screen (hide the code), so his eyes can only reach the app buttons.
  • No internet is needed to use the app.

7. Lock the iPad to the app (Guided Access)

  • Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access → on, and set a passcode.
  • In Guided Access settings, set Display Auto-Lock to Never.
  • Open Swift Playgrounds, run the YES NO app full screen, then press the top (or Home) button three times → Start.
  • To leave: press three times again and enter the passcode.

How he uses it

Home screen: looking around is safe. Looking at TALK (top middle) opens YES/NO – he wants to say something. Then ask him a yes/no question straight away: the buttons are dimmed for 3 seconds, and after that wherever his eyes rest can count as an answer. He looks at YES or NO. The iPad says the word and stays on YES/NO, so you can ask the next question (after each answer it waits 3 seconds, or the time set in Settings). EXIT goes back to Home at any time. If he chooses nothing for 60 seconds, it goes back to Home by itself.

Settings (for the family)

On the Home screen, touch and hold the small grey ⚙️ Settings button (bottom right) for 1 second. A short tap does nothing, so his eyes cannot open it. Pause between answers: how long the app waits after an answer (0 to 10 seconds, normally 3). Under 3 seconds, one long look may give the same answer twice. TRAINING button: see below. Tap Done to go back.

TRAINING (practice with pictures)

Put pictures he likes in one folder on the iPad (Files app). In Settings turn the TRAINING button on, tap Choose folder and pick that folder (only once; the app remembers it). Done works only when the folder has pictures. Now the Home screen has a purple 🖼️ TRAINING button (top right) next to TALK. He chooses himself: TALK = normal YES/NO with voice, TRAINING = practice with pictures. In training: he looks at YES → a picture fills the YES place for 15 seconds. Then only NO works → the next picture fills the NO place for 15 seconds. They take turns, always starting with YES. The button that does not work at the moment is dimmed. Pictures come in random order; each is shown once before any repeats. There is no sound in training. EXIT always works and goes back to Home, where he can choose TALK or TRAINING again. To hide the TRAINING button, turn it off in Settings.
